00

The whole pipeline

A calling travels nine stages. Every stage either waits on a person or fires on its own — and the difference matters when something looks stuck.

Reading the column codes

Throughout this document, a code like col AO is the column in the Calling Approvals sheet where that fact gets recorded. If you ever need to know whether a step really happened, that column is the source of truth — the dashboard is only a view of it.

—

Who does what

Six roles touch the pipeline. Most people only ever see the two or three stages that belong to them.

RoleThey act atWhat lands on their dashboard
Bishop / ward leader Stage 1 Nothing. They submit the form and are done — the pipeline reports back through their ward clerk's summary email.
Stake Presidency Stages 3, 4, 7 Approvals to vote on, callings to extend, setting-apart assignments. The president also sees who is still outstanding on every pending item.
High Council Stages 3, 4, 6, 7 Approvals, extends for their assigned ward, the Sunday stake-business confirmation and sustaining report, and setting aparts.
Executive Secretary Stages 2, 4 Interviews to schedule, appointments to book, and the Schedule/Assign bin — deciding who extends a calling.
Stake Clerk Stages 2, 8, 9 Clerk reviews that block polling, the Monday unconfirmed-sustainings check, and CC on the certificate chain.
Membership Clerk Stages 2, 9 Clerk reviews, LCR records, and all four ordination-certificate steps.
01

Submitting

One form, three destinations. What the form asks changes with who is filling it out and what they picked on the first page.

Everything enters through the same public form. The first question — calling, release, or ordination — decides which pages you see and which sheet the submission lands in.

Who can fill it out

The second page asks "Your Calling." That list is fixed, but it is not the same for every submission type — a release or an ordination offers a shorter list than a calling recommendation.

One question can change the whole path

The release flow opens with a pre-flight: is someone being called to replace them? Answer yes and the form quietly switches to a calling recommendation — the longer submitter list comes back, and the release is handled as part of that calling rather than on its own. You only stay on the release path when nobody is replacing them.

Picking your calling doesn't just label the submission — it decides which callings you may recommend at all, and whether you're offered the "on behalf of" page. Both come from the Callings Chart, per calling, so the form adapts without anyone editing it.

The questions about the member

Two requirement questions can appear, and only when that specific calling requires them. Every answer is recorded, including the uncertain ones — "Unsure" is a valid answer, and routing it to a clerk is the whole point.

When no priesthood question is asked

For callings with no priesthood requirement, that same column records gender instead — the form asks when it needs to, and a Relief Society calling is set to Female automatically without asking at all. One column, two meanings, depending on the calling.

The High Priest case

A calling that requires a High Priest never fails the requirement check, even when the member is currently an Elder. Instead the ordination rides along with the calling: approvers see the note "By approving you also sustain them to be ordained a High Priest," and the item is read in stake business as Sustain & Ordain.

This is why a high council calling for a brother who isn't yet a High Priest moves through the pipeline normally rather than stalling.

The other questions

  • Consultation — some callings name a leader who must be consulted first. If you are not that leader, the form asks you to confirm you consulted them before it lets you continue.
  • Stake interview — asked when the calling requires one. Answering Needs to be scheduled or Unsure creates a task for the executive secretaries, but unlike the requirement questions it does not hold polling — approval and interview scheduling proceed in parallel.
  • Who is being released — naming a person here is what creates the matching release task later. A second, unrelated release can also be added from the same page.
  • Comments — free text. A comment normally holds polling for a clerk to read first.
  • Stake presidency options — shown only to the stake president, presidency, clerks and executive secretaries. On a calling you tick each presidency member who has already approved, so only the rest are polled; on a release it's a single yes/no about the stake president.
A shortcut worth knowing

When a clerk or secretary leaves a comment on their own submission, they can leave "Hold polling until a clerk reviews this comment" unchecked. Polling then starts immediately and no review task is created — otherwise they'd be assigning themselves the job of reading their own comment.

02

The clerk review gate

One column decides whether a submission polls tonight or waits for a clerk. This is the single most common reason something "isn't moving."

Every row carries a Poll? flag in col R. The hourly job only picks up rows where it is TRUE. Four things can set it false at submission, and each one creates a task for the clerks.

If something seems stuck at this stage

Open the Clerk Reviews section of the dashboard — an unanswered review is almost always the cause. The row is fine; it simply has col R = FALSE and the hourly job is skipping it by design.

03

Polling for approval

Fifteen approval columns — three for the presidency, twelve for the high council. How they get filled depends on one setting and on what the submitter already recorded.

When the hourly job first picks up a row, it stamps a marker in col B so it is never polled twice, writes a status into col C, and notifies whoever is being asked. There are four ways this plays out.

Checking how this stake is currently set to poll…

Casting a vote

Approvers see pending items at the top of their dashboard and answer Approve or Concern. There is also an Approve All button, which is the intended way to work through a batch — voting one at a time on a slow connection is what used to make the dashboard feel unresponsive.

Clerks and secretaries can record a vote on behalf of someone who gave it verbally. That runs exactly the same completion logic as a self-vote, so a recorded approval moves the item forward identically.

Concern is not a "no"

Raising a concern opens the opposition flow rather than declining the calling. It is a signal to the presidency, not a final answer — the item stays in the pipeline while it is worked out.

04

Extending the calling

The moment the last approval lands, the system decides who should extend the calling — and for one category, it deliberately declines to decide.

Each calling on the Callings Chart carries an Extend Call value that names who does the extending. That value fans out to six outcomes.

What the extender sees

The assigned person gets an Extend calling task; if someone is being released from that position, a separate Extend release task is created alongside it, because the two conversations can happen at different times with different people. All three presidency members, the clerks and the secretaries are CC'd, so anyone can see the state and any of them can act.

Accepted

The calling moves on

Recorded in col AJ. The row immediately becomes eligible for the next stake business meeting.

Declined

The pipeline stops

Recorded in col AX. Nothing further is created — the presidency decides what to do next.

Release extended

Tracked separately

Recorded in col AL. A release can be extended before, after, or without the matching calling.

Sent to the bin

Reassign without losing it

Any extend task can be pushed back into the Schedule/Assign bin — useful when the assigned person can't reach the member.

05

Onto stake business

Once accepted, an item queues for a Sunday. Which Sunday depends on the clock — specifically, on that ward's sacrament meeting time.

An accepted calling is added to the Stake Business sheet for an upcoming Sunday. The date is chosen per ward: if the ward's meeting hasn't started yet, the item goes on this Sunday; if that meeting has already passed, it rolls to the next one. Stake-wide items use the earliest meeting time across all wards.

06

Sunday sustaining

Sunday morning the system builds one report per ward for whoever is conducting. Marking an item there is what actually advances the calling.

At 8am each Sunday, every ward with business gets a sustaining report assigned to whoever is conducting — a high councilor, a specific counselor, or the whole presidency. Releases are listed first, matching how they're read in the meeting.

What happens when the conductor marks an item depends on whether it was sustained in one ward or across the whole stake.

Monday morning

Anything left unmarked from Sunday is collected into an unconfirmed sustainings review for the clerks, who can push each item to the next Sunday or dismiss it. Only items that had a report task appear — if a ward's availability was never confirmed, there was no report to leave unmarked.

07

Setting apart

Who sets a person apart is a property of the calling, not of who extended it.

The Callings Chart names the setter for each calling, and the task is created automatically the moment the sustaining is recorded. Four routings exist:

SP

The stake president

He is the sole primary on the task.

SPC

The counselor over that ward

Resolved from the ward's high council assignment.

HC

The high councilor over that ward

The same person who covers that ward's stake business.

SPC / HC

Both, together

The counselor and the ward's high councilor are both primaries — either completes it.

In every case the full presidency, the clerks, the secretaries and the conducting high councilor are CC'd, so the task is visible to everyone who might need to act on it. Completion is recorded in col AR.

Members outside the stake

When someone called to a stake position lives in a ward outside the stake, a second out-of-unit record task is created alongside the setting apart. Submitting that short form emails the member's details and the ward-clerk instructions to the stake and membership clerks — the two tasks complete independently, because the ordinance and the paperwork rarely happen the same day.

08

Records, emails and name tags

The quiet tail of the pipeline — mostly automatic, and mostly invisible unless you're a clerk.

Ward clerk summary emails

Rather than emailing a ward clerk once per person, the system queues each sustaining and sends one summary per ward on the hourly run. The email names the setter, and adds reminders about a secondary approval or keys when that calling needs them.

Name tags

Callings flagged as needing a name tag appear in a dedicated section for the executive secretaries once the calling has been accepted. Marking one ordered stamps col AT.

What stays manual

Two things are deliberately left to a person: recording the ordinance on LCR, and the ratification step on ordinations. The dashboard tracks that they need doing but never claims they're done on its own.

R

The release-only path

A release with no replacement takes a deliberately short route — one approver, no requirements, no interview.

O

The ordination path

Ordinations run on their own sheet and in a different order — the interview comes first, and a certificate chain follows at the end.

The important structural difference: a calling is approved and then extended, but a brother is interviewed first and only sustained afterward. That's why the ordination form never asks about presidency approval — at submission there is nothing to pre-record.

✻

Pause, hold, cancel — and undo

Three different ways to stop something, which are easy to confuse. They stop different amounts of the pipeline.

ControlWhat it stopsWhat keeps runningUndoing it
Pause Everything. Approvals, extends, stake business, tasks — all hidden and halted. Nothing. Resume puts it back exactly where it was.
Hold Only entry into stake business. Approvals and extending continue normally. Release hold — it joins the next stake business run.
Cancel Everything, and signals the calling isn't happening. Nothing. Resume is still possible; nothing is deleted.

All three surface in the Paused / Held / Canceled Callings section, which is where you go to bring something back. Nothing is ever deleted — a paused calling's tasks disappear from view and reappear intact when it resumes.

Undo

Most actions — approvals, accepts, sustainings, setting aparts — are logged and can be reversed from Recent Actions on the dashboard. Undo reverses the record and cancels whatever downstream tasks that action created. The one thing it can't clean up automatically is a stake-wide "wards reported" entry; it warns you when that's the case.

⏱

What runs on a clock

Nothing here needs a person to press a button. Knowing the cadence explains most "why hasn't it moved yet" questions.

WhenWhat happens
Every hourNew submissions start polling · approvals are reconciled · ward clerk summary emails go out · duplicate tasks are cleaned up
ThursdayHigh councilors are asked to confirm availability · the stake business document is created and posted to the high council channel
Sunday, 8amSustaining reports are built and assigned to whoever is conducting in each ward
Monday morningAnything left unmarked from Sunday becomes an unconfirmed-sustainings review for the clerks
NightlyOld completed and acknowledged tasks are purged on their retention schedules
Why an approval sometimes takes an hour to appear

Voting is instant for the person voting. But a vote that completes a row — the last approval, say — triggers work that is deliberately deferred a couple of seconds so the tap returns quickly, and some reconciliation only runs on the hourly job.

So an extend task might appear a few seconds after the final approval, or on the next hour if something was interrupted. If an item looks stuck for more than an hour, that's worth investigating; a few minutes is normal.

Urgent vs standard

Every submission carries a priority. Standard follows each approver's own notification preferences — some want an immediate message, some a daily digest. Urgent overrides that and notifies everyone immediately.

Use urgent for callings that genuinely can't wait. Its value comes from being rare.
